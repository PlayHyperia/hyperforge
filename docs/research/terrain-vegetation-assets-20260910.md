# Compact Island Landscape, Vegetation, and Asset Production

## Assessment

The most useful next step is a composed environment vertical slice, not a renderer replacement or an indiscriminate increase in detail. Hyperia has a usable foundation: deterministic authored terrain, retained rendered-surface sampling, material-preserving instancing, bounded asynchronous grass installation, and real capture receipts. The missing ingredient is a coherent landscape that reads well from the actual arena broadcast and preparation cameras.

The intended world is one compact island organized around **one duel arena**, with gathering and preparation visibly connected to that arena. **The existing choppable resource trees are the island's tree population. Redistribute and improve those functional trees; do not add a separate decorative tree population.** The retired large island is not a second playable target. A premium result depends on recognizable silhouettes, believable material transitions, grouped vegetation, readable activity spaces, consistent lighting, and stable motion. “AAA” is an art and experience target here, not evidence that a browser has the same rendering budget or proprietary systems as a console title.

**Recommendation:** establish the single-arena composition first, then finish one pond–workshop–bank–arena route with its background tree line. Lock that route as the visual benchmark before expanding its palette across the compact island. Terrain-contact tests remain mandatory, but a numerically correct, sparsely dressed brown bowl is not an art pass.

## Landscape composition and readable gameplay

**External evidence.** Sucker Punch’s environment-art lead describes limiting foliage variety within biomes, reducing texture noise, and using a controlled color palette to make spaces memorable. Its procedural tools enabled artistic iteration instead of replacing direction. Guerrilla’s published placement system similarly connects artist-authored rules, tree lines, roads, and terrain. These are strong examples of controlled procedural production, not an instruction to reproduce either game’s art or engine. [1](https://blog.playstation.com/2020/07/09/crafting-the-world-of-tsushima/) [2](https://www.guerrilla-games.com/read/gpu-based-procedural-placement-in-horizon-zero-dawn)

**Recommendation: one spatial hierarchy.** The arena should be the primary built landmark; the preparation workshop and bank should be the secondary activity cluster; the pond and resource groves should provide distinctive natural pockets. The ridge and canopy form a quiet background, not competing monuments. Give each area a different silhouette and ground treatment while keeping one shared regional palette.

Start with three designed camera compositions: the arena broadcast, an agent’s preparation route, and an island overview. Draw their sightline corridors and movement corridors before placing major trees or roofs. Keep the central combat plane visually calm enough to distinguish agent facing, weapon action, projectile travel, and health-state effects. Frame it with trees and architecture outside the active silhouettes; do not place attractive foliage in front of the information viewers need.

The route should reveal why preparation matters: pond/resource edge → supplies and workshop → bank/loadout → arena. That does not require making every activity visible simultaneously. A few connected views with recognizable landmarks are preferable to an aerial shot of many identical courts or a panorama filled with unrelated props. Any removal of the other courts must update authoritative arena allocation, spawn/restore rules, navigation, spectator targets, and content identity together—not only hide five meshes.

**Landform recommendation.** Author a small number of broad shapes: a sheltered preparation bench, a low enclosing ridge, one open coastal view, an asymmetric pond shoulder, and an arena terrace. Preserve a continuous slope language between them. Build geological features from a few shared outcrop forms with consistent layering and weathering; avoid equal spacing, uniform rock sizes, and a decorative ring following the water mathematically.

Use large shapes to make the location attractive when viewed as a grayscale thumbnail. Use medium details to explain use and ecology: worn path edges, work areas, root clusters, shallow banks, and tree groups. Reserve fine texture and small props for places that cameras actually approach. This three-scale hierarchy is an art recommendation, not a numerical guarantee of quality.

## Terrain materials and surface continuity

**External facts.** glTF specifies a metallic–roughness material model, tangent-space normal maps, and explicit alpha behavior. Three.js uses a linear working color space while color textures require appropriate color-space annotation; roughness, normal, and other data maps must not be interpreted as display color. [3](https://registry.khronos.org/glTF/specs/2.0/glTF-2.0.html) [4](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r183/manual/en/color-management.html)

**Recommendation: keep the existing three-layer compact material and improve its authored distribution first.** Grass/soil/rock should describe a place, not merely color three slope ranges. Use the current world-space control fields for path wear, wetness, grades, and biome transitions. The pond bank should be damp closest to the water, not dark mud all the way up a dry shelf. Rock should occur in plausible exposures and continue into the selected boulder family. Grass should remain visible in undisturbed areas around the workshop, without covering work footprints or agent routes.

There are three distinct problems to review independently:

- **Macro distribution:** where ground families occur over tens of metres, and how their colors support the view.
- **Meso structure:** path edges, banks, outcrops, erosion, roots, and transitions over metres.
- **Micro response:** normal strength, roughness, pores, pebbles, and filtering at close range.

Adding high-frequency noise does not repair the first two. The current material already has blended projections and controlled normal fading. First compare it at the actual camera heights and output resolution. A visible repetition problem should be localized before adding a more complex stochastic shader.

**Research option, not the immediate milestone.** Heitz and Neyret describe histogram-preserving patch blending for stochastic textures; Deliot and Heitz later address implementation cost, mip-level color deviations, and compressed textures. This is relevant if ordinary repeated ground tiles remain obvious. It adds preprocessing and shader work and is not a ready-made Three.js terrain feature. A bounded TSL experiment on one ground family should retain the present shader as a measured baseline. Do not apply three-patch synthesis to every layer and projection without counting the resulting samples. [5](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/722-2/) [6](https://eheitzresearch.wordpress.com/738-2/)

Keep visual microdetail out of authoritative height unless it changes actual traversal. A normal map may imply small roughness without moving the collision plane; a large displaced bank cannot. Existing Float32 triangle anchoring for grass and full-footprint floor checks are valuable precisely because the rendered mesh is a discretization of the authoritative function. Any new terrain displacement requires the same CPU, worker, mesh, water, and collision contract—not a GPU-only cosmetic fix beneath agents.

**Specific review checklist:** inspect one neutral-light material swatch, one grazing-angle path shot, one shadowed bank, one wide overview, and the compressed stream. Check texture scale against an avatar, normal orientation, dry/wet roughness contrast, grass-to-soil hue continuity, and over-sharpening. Track exposure and lighting separately so palette edits do not compensate for a lighting defect.

## Trees, shrubs, and groundcover

The task is to compose and spread the existing choppable trees across the compact island, using those same trees to form its groves, background canopy, and landmarks. Keep the existing species' skill requirements and resource outputs meaningful. A coherent palette does not require collapsing progression species into two interchangeable resources: select compatible visual variants and materials within the current definitions, keeping advanced trees recognizable. Understory shrubs, the approved fern, restrained reeds, and rocks can support the composition without creating another population of non-harvestable trees.

**Placement recommendation:** manually author a few habitat anchors and let deterministic rules fill their surroundings. Taller trees belong on the outer ridge or rear edges; understory groups belong in sheltered pockets; reeds belong at shallow water margins. Keep intentional gaps where cameras see agents and where resources must be approached. Random yaw and scale alone do not create convincing ecology: clustering, age/size distribution, shared orientation on exposed slopes, and empty ground between groups matter.

Every tree in that composition retains its authoritative resource entity, interaction, and collision footprint. Preserve reachable adjacent harvesting tiles and clearance for agent routes; canopy framing must not hide the trunk or agent being followed. A fern should not acquire a large navigation blocker just because its crown bounds are broad. Store separate semantic support, collision, interaction, and culling extents; they solve different problems.

The recent pond fix demonstrates this distinction. Rocks retain conservative complete-footprint grounding. Fern, bush, and reed support is derived from finite lower geometry within a 0.18 m central radius and the lowest 0.05 m slice; their entire crown remains in culling bounds. The canonical fern has 31 selected support vertices, bush 6, and reeds 184. These measurements establish a support contract, not universal botanical truth or proof that every visible leaf touches ground. Different source models may need explicit author-provided root metadata instead of that particular heuristic.

`gathering/woodcutting.json` contains 12 definitions and 47 LOD0 variant paths: pine(5), oak(2), maple(4), palm(5), banana(5), dead(4), pineDead(3), bamboo(4), eucalyptus(5), general(6), magic(2), mahogany(2). All currently specify a four-tick roll cycle, 0.125 depletion chance, and 80-tick respawn; at the 600 ms game tick that means 2.4 s between rolls and a 48 s depletion interval. Success probability and tool/level validation remain separate from roll frequency. Outputs are logs, oak logs, maple logs, mahogany logs, or magic logs with their existing skill rewards. No local `packages/server/manifests/gathering/woodcutting.json` override exists in this inspected checkout; DataManager supports such an override, so qualification must record which manifest was actually consumed.

**Lifecycle and authority.** Terrain emits `RESOURCE_SPAWN_POINTS_REGISTERED`; `ResourceSystem` resolves the species manifest, snaps X/Z, rejects positions inside the overall duel-arena zone, hydrates persisted depletion, and creates `ResourceEntity`. Authored world-area resources are protected from terrain-unload removal. Procedural resources are removed with their owning terrain tile, including entity, active gathering presentation, and timer indexes. Server gathering validates tool, skill, position and inventory, and publishes committed rewards through the existing durable reward path. Depletion marks the entity unavailable, broadcasts state and schedules tick-based respawn. Respawn restores availability and broadcasts it. `ResourceEntity` registers its navigation blocking tiles on the server and removes them on destruction; deplete/respawn do not remove/re-add those blockers.

The current manifests select `TreeGLBVisualStrategy` → `GLBTreeBatchedInstancer`, sharing variant/material pools. Variant choice is hashed from the entity ID; visual yaw also incorporates its position. Client modification drives depletion/reappearance through the instancer. The current tree strategy handles depletion itself and does **not** load the manifest's separate stump model; `GPUMaterials` uses screen-door depletion with `DISSOLVE_ALPHA_SCALE=0.7`. Therefore “stump replacement looks correct” is not an established behavior. A visible depleted state that remains understandable beside an unchanged collision footprint needs an actual harvest-cycle review.

**Concrete redistribution hazards and missing acceptance.**

- The generator rejects water/low clearance, roads, excessive slope, and unsuitable species heights. Registration additionally rejects the whole duel-arena bounds. Neither path proves that a snapped trunk leaves a reachable neighboring harvesting tile or avoids every station approach, building footprint, other resource footprint, and camera corridor. Use one authoritative compact placement validation pass, not client-only tree movement.
- Sampling happens before X/Z snapping, while `snapToTileCenter` preserves Y. Recompute/check height and dry-land clearance at the final admitted position; the difference is especially relevant on banks and slopes. This is a source-level mismatch, not a measured floating-tree claim.
- Generated local coordinates are in `[0, tileSize)` and are added to `tileIndex*tileSize`; unload ownership uses centered terrain tiles, `floor((position + tileSize/2)/tileSize)`. Verify/correct this ownership contract before broad redistribution: a generated point in the upper half maps to a different centered tile. Existing intratile Poisson spacing also does not establish a global minimum distance between independent tile RNGs or between generated and explicit trees.
- Persistent resource IDs are derived from type and rounded snapped coordinates, not the incoming spawn ID or species. Relocating a tree changes its persisted key; placing two trees in one snapped tile aliases that key. Preserve IDs for retained placements, reject duplicates, and explicitly version/migrate any relocated nodes during a drained, non-active cycle. Never silently reset depletion or active reward custody to obtain a prettier layout.
- Current resource LOD thresholds exceed most compact views, and spectator/background-LOD policy may retain LOD0 alone. More trees are not free merely because variants are batched; count submitted triangles, material batches, alpha/shadow work, downloads and texture residency on the actual selected path.

**Smallest safe implementation plan.** First export a deterministic census of explicit plus generated admitted trees: species, stable ID, owning tile, snapped position, height, footprint, reachability, active/depleted state and selected visual variant. Then compose two or three asymmetrical resource-grove regions spread across the usable island, with smaller connecting groups and clear activity routes. Keep early-level wood accessible to preparation, retain reachable advanced species, and use the rear/outer groves to frame the single arena without obscuring combat. Start by redistributing the existing admitted count and species mix; assess whether a bounded count increase is actually needed only after those views. Use the same server resource entities and instancers, never decorative copies. Preserve resource yields, skill thresholds, timing and custody semantics.

Existing tests include `BiomeResourceSpawning.test.ts` (real generator with controlled callbacks), `ResourceSystem.terrainLifecycle.test.ts` (authored retention, bounded chunk churn), `ResourceSystem.landEcology.test.ts` (25-agent/60,000-tick simulation), `ResourceSystem.rewardCustody.test.ts`, and `ResourceSystem.inventoryPersistence.integration.test.ts`. The latter uses real system classes with persistence fixtures; its filename is not evidence of a live production database test. None was rerun for this research update. Add real admitted-world census/order/duplicate/ownership tests, actual navigation from bank/forge to every harvesting adjacency, concurrent chop/deplete/respawn/reload/late-join verification, and a visual cycle at near and broadcast distances. Keep long-duration resource availability and durable exactly-once rewards as acceptance gates while changing layout.

### Wind and foliage shading

**External evidence.** Tiago Sousa’s Crytek chapter separates overall plant bending from leaf-detail motion, uses artist-controlled stiffness and phase data, and warns against implausible deformation. Its shading architecture is dated and engine-specific; the useful transferable principle is hierarchical, artist-controlled motion. Eric Wohllaib’s original GDC talk describes procedural grass blades with individual appearance and animation under memory/performance constraints. Neither provides a browser frame-rate promise. [7](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis) [8](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027033/Advanced-Graphics-Summit-Procedural-Grass)

**Recommendation:** coordinate low-cost wind direction and gust phase across grass, reeds, leaves, and appropriate cloth. The actual resource-tree material already has wind uniforms and vertex-color leaf masking; inspect and qualify that existing behavior rather than replacing it based only on the grass implementation. Keep trunks and roots still, vary stiffness by plant and height, and use small secondary leaf motion. Motion should remain recognizable in the streamed video without shimmering or dominating combat.

Use cutout foliage with deliberate alpha coverage and enough geometry to retain silhouette. Instancing reduces draw submission, not shaded pixels; overlapping double-sided leaf cards can remain expensive. Three.js exposes alpha-to-coverage, but it requires multisampling; the installed WebGPU pipeline gates it on a sample count greater than one. Test the actual render target, alpha cutoff, mip chain, shadows, and streamed motion. Do not assume that setting a material flag proves improved foliage. [9](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/Material.html)

## Grass coverage, LOD, and instancing

**Inference from the local profile:** a 14 m candidate lattice is too sparse to communicate a continuous grassy bank in the retained preparation views. The evidence is stronger than an impression: probe16 observes no grass anchors in two requested regions. This does not justify enabling the expensive player-height tier across the entire island.

**Recommendation: define coverage from camera roles and ground regions.** The close preparation route needs readable grass clumps and soft ground transitions. The arena needs shorter, calmer edges and an uncluttered combat plane. The distant ridge needs a coherent ground color and broad plant masses, not tiny independently rendered blades. Keep the current authored-surface, elevated-water, road, and exclusion rules; change distribution deliberately rather than bypassing them to satisfy a count.

An initial A/B test could use a 3 m candidate spacing in a selected preparation corridor and5–7m in its middle distance, retaining the cheaper clump geometry until images show it is inadequate. Beyond useful screen size, fade to ground color instead of preserving blades solely because they remain within the island bounds. These are test parameters, not launch settings. For illustration, an 8,000 m² eligible region has approximately 889 lattice candidates at 3 m spacing versus 41 at 14 m, before masks and jitter. The increase is substantial and must be measured.

**External facts.** Three.js instanced meshes share geometry/materials while providing per-instance transforms. Khronos explicitly warns that a giant instance batch can undermine frustum culling and recommends spatially colocated batches for worlds. The glTF instancing extension is not necessary to obtain GPU instancing in an application. [10](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html) [11](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/tree/main/extensions/2.0/Vendor/EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing)

**Recommendation:** preserve resource-tree material/variant batching and measure its actual culling and upload behavior. Its pools are organized by tree type and material/LOD, not the decorative system's 64 m cells. If hidden groups measurably waste work, a spatial partition must preserve resource IDs, highlighting, collision proxies, depletion state, and teardown; it is a scoped change, not a configuration switch. Small batches improve culling but increase draw calls. Choose LOD transitions using projected silhouette error, with distance as a convenient approximation. A 120 m tree threshold may suit one silhouette and not another; blanket 800 m settings are not an art target.

Do not enable disabled impostors merely to check off an optimization. First test real LOD1/LOD2 files for material parity, canopy silhouette, shadow transition, and hysteresis. If the whole relevant island is visible and distant trees still dominate cost, qualify impostors as a separate feature with consistent lighting and camera orbit tests. Keep full-resolution geometry for the small number of foreground trees that establish scale.

Grass performance acceptance must include actual eligible area, unique installed anchors, visible coverage, worker latency, upload duration, rendered triangles, shadow work, and frame-time percentiles. A record of zero grounding violations is valuable but insufficient. Record moving footage at the final stream resolution to find aliasing and temporal instability that a still image cannot expose.

## Texture and geometry delivery

**External facts.** KTX2/Basis textures can be transcoded to device-supported compressed GPU formats; PNG/JPEG/WebP file size alone does not express texture residency. Khronos recommends choosing codecs according to content and checking artifacts. Normal and packed data maps often need more conservative compression than color maps. Full mip chains, correct color/data metadata, and dimensions divisible by four are important constraints for `KHR_texture_basisu`. [12](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/3D-Formats-Guidelines/refs/heads/main/KTXArtistGuide.md) [13](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_texture_basisu/README.md)

The compact terrain’s six 1024² RGBA8 textures imply approximately 32 MiB including full mip chains, independent of their PNG download size. That is an arithmetic estimate, not a WebGPU allocation measurement. Block compression can reduce resident storage, but the actual format, fallback, mip levels, staging allocations, and driver behavior must be recorded. A fourfold reduction is not guaranteed on every device.

**Implementation recommendation:** add one owned KTX2 loader after renderer initialization, detect that renderer’s support, configure the transcoder location/worker limit, and attach it to every relevant GLTFLoader path—including retries. The installed ModelCache already configures Meshopt but does not configure KTX2. Terrain maps use a separate custom loader and therefore need their own deliberate integration; adding GLB support will not compress those six maps automatically. Preserve abort, late-result disposal, hash admission, color-space/alpha behavior, and shared-resource ownership. Three.js’s r183 KTX2 source documents the renderer initialization/support sequence. [14](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/r183/examples/jsm/loaders/KTX2Loader.js)

Run a single approved fern and one terrain layer through PNG-versus-KTX2 comparisons before converting the library. Inspect leaf edges, distant mip coverage, roughness bands, normal direction, cold-start latency, and hot-reload teardown. Prefer UASTC candidates for sensitive normal/packed maps and compare ETC1S/UASTC for albedo; do not make this a universal preset. An artist’s original lossless master should remain available.

Meshopt is already wired locally. Compression, quantization, and simplification are distinct operations: a smaller encoded buffer is not necessarily fewer rendered triangles, and simplification can damage branch silhouettes or attachment metadata. gltfpack and glTF Transform expose different operations; the latter explicitly recommends inspecting an asset and selecting optimizations instead of assuming its general-purpose defaults suit every scene. [15](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zeux/meshoptimizer/master/gltf/README.md) [16](https://gltf-transform.dev/cli)

Both EXT and KHR Meshopt extension names are present in the installed GLTFLoader. The online KHR specification currently labels itself a release candidate. Keep the existing accepted format during this art slice; changing encoder/extension versions is an independent compatibility test, not an automatic upgrade. Verify the emitted bitstream with the installed decoder and actual loader before promotion. [17](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF/blob/main/extensions/2.0/Khronos/KHR_meshopt_compression/README.md)

| Terrain refinement | Retain local 128 pond grids and existing coarse/fine policy; no global density rise | Actual triangle error/contact, seam/floor coverage, visible improvement, exact geometry-memory delta |
| Grass coverage experiment | Only named preparation/arena-edge regions; bounded installs and retained revision checks | Nonzero regional coverage, eligible-area density, stream footage, worker/upload/frame-time receipts |
| Full scene | Select minimum supported hardware and output resolution before hard caps | Target stable 60 fps where required; 16.67 ms total frame interval, with margin. Report p50/p95/p99 and thermal steady state—not a screenshot FPS number |

CPU, GPU, delivery, and art are separate gates. A fast worker can still cause upload stalls; a low triangle count can still produce expensive alpha overdraw; compressed source files can still occupy large GPU textures. Include camera sweeps and fast transitions between preparation and arena, cold loading and warm revisits, camera-range re-entry, scene destroy/recreate, and the actual video encoding workload.

Khronos’s validator provides structural, binary, image, and reference checks with machine-readable results. Use it alongside project checks, not as proof of appearance, performance, navigation, or complete extension behavior. [26](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/glTF-Validator/blob/main/README.md)

## Ordered implementation scope

1. **Single-arena composition:** establish authoritative one-arena layout and actual broadcast/preparation sightlines; remove obsolete playable-court assumptions coherently. Approve the landform and large silhouettes before dressing.
2. **Functional resource-tree composition:** inventory and redistribute existing choppable trees across the compact island. Use the existing ResourceSystem/GLB tree-instancer path; preserve species progression, reachable harvesting, persistence, depletion/respawn, route clearance, and camera readability. No separate decorative-tree population. Qualify source provenance and actual LODs before increasing count.
3. **Preparation ground coverage:** fix the deliberate sparse streaming profile for selected regions; keep authored terrain/exclusion/water rules. Compare regional density and actual streamed images, not only anchor correctness.
4. **Surface art pass:** tune existing three material families, paths, bank transitions, and outcrop continuity under fixed lighting. Add assets only to solve a visible compositional gap.
5. **Motion and alpha pass:** coherent wind, root-stable foliage, compatible shadows, mip/alpha review, and encoded-video inspection.
6. **Delivery/performance pass:** qualify one KTX2 material and one Meshopt/LOD workflow before batch conversion; profile cold loads, memory, frame-time tails, and long-running transitions.
7. **Whole-island acceptance:** repeat the approved style across the compact world, preserving material/asset budgets and gameplay access. Require both human visual approval and technical gates for every fixed stream view plus the route cameras.

The next visible milestone should be a complete, attractive journey between preparation and the single arena. Further numerical fixes remain necessary when they reveal defects, but should support that milestone rather than substitute for it.
